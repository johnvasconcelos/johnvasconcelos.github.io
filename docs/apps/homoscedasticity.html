Homoscedasticity: why the residuals become one normal

Model: Yi = β0 + β1xi + εi, with εi ~ N(0, σ²). Each observation has its own normal distribution standing upright around the line.

Subtracts ŷ from each point: Y − ŷ = residual.
At 90° you look straight at the curves, along the x axis.
Homoscedastic: same σ for every x.
regression line (OLS)
sum (average) of all the normals
reference normal N(0, σ²)

How to use it in class

  1. Starting view. Each point has a normal centered on its fitted value ŷi. Under homoscedasticity they all have the same width: only the center moves along the line.
  2. Slider 1. Subtracting ŷ brings every normal down to zero. What is left are the residuals ei = Yi − ŷi, all centered on 0.
  3. Slider 2. Rotating to 90° makes the x axis vanish into depth, and the curves fall on top of each other. If σ is constant they match exactly: a single N(0, σ²). The residual histogram appears behind them for comparison.
  4. Contrast. Switch to "Grows with x" and repeat. Now the stacked curves have different widths; their sum (orange) is more peaked with heavier tails than the reference normal (dashed). The stacked residuals do not form a single normal with variance σ².
